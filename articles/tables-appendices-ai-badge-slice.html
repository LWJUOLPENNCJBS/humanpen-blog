Tables, appendices, and captions often sit outside the AI writing percentage—read the badge as a slice

Charles Li / HumanPen

When an AI writing percentage appears on Moodle, Canvas, or Blackboard, many students in India and Southeast Asia treat it like a grade for the entire submission: cover page, tables, reference list, appendix protocols, figure captions, and all. That is usually the wrong mental model. Most institutional pipelines estimate AI writing patterns on qualifying continuous prose. Large stretches of your file may never enter the numerator or the denominator the way your essay body does.

This post is about partial-coverage literacy—how to read a percentage when tables and appendices are effectively unscored. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Why the badge can ignore the parts you spent days on

Reference lists, numbered tables, short captions, bullet-heavy appendices, and formula blocks often contribute little or no continuous prose under a given tool’s rules. The card may still show a confident-looking percentage for the introduction and literature review while your methods appendix sits gray, blank, or simply absent from the highlight map.

That asymmetry produces two opposite panics in WhatsApp groups:

  • “My appendix is clean, so the 24% must be wrong.”
  • “My appendix is huge, so the tool must have scored everything.”

Neither claim follows. The percentage answers a narrower question: among the text the tool treated as scorable continuous prose in this upload, how much looked model-like under this estimator?

A practical map of “in” vs “out”

Often in continuous-prose scoringOften outside or lightly scoredWhat to do
Introduction, lit review, discussion paragraphsStandalone tables / spreadsheet dumpsCheck the highlight map, not the badge
Narrative methods paragraphsBullet protocols, checklistsAsk whether your school wants prose or appendices revised
Long figure explanations written as paragraphsOne-line captionsDo not “pad” captions to chase a number
Abstract (when long enough)Reference list / bibliographyKeep citations frozen unless factually wrong

Product details differ. Your job is not to reverse-engineer a vendor. Your job is to open the full report and see which pages actually carry highlights.

Incomplete indicators make partial coverage worse

If the card also shows `\*%`, a gray “unprocessed” strip, or a confidence band, the headline number may be both partial (not whole-file) and conditional (not finished). Refresh, download the official export, and read footnotes before you rewrite. Students in Bengaluru, Singapore, and Jakarta lose hours arguing about a badge that was still processing.

Similarity still answers a different question

A low similarity score does not cancel an AI writing indicator. An AI writing indicator does not prove plagiarism. Partial coverage makes both instruments easier to misread, because a classmate’s appendix-heavy PDF is not a fair comparison for your prose-heavy DOCX.

What to bring to office hours

Bring: full report (with footnotes), the exact attempt file, a one-page map of highlighted vs gray vs never-shown sections, and the module clause on AI writing tools. Ask clearly: “For this assignment, should revision focus on flagged continuous prose only?” That question is more useful than “Is 24% bad?”

If revision is required, confirm the file identity in writing, then edit flagged passages first. Freeze tables, citations, and checked facts unless they are wrong.

Short notes

  • Treat the AI writing badge as a slice of continuous prose, not a whole-file grade.
  • Tables and appendices can be “invisible” to the percentage and still matter for assessment.
  • No shortcut worth writing about is a “guaranteed pass.”
