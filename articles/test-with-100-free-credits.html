100 free credits on signup, credits never expire: run the loop once before paying

Charles Li / HumanPen · pricing · 8 Nov 2026

Every tool claims a free trial. The useful question is whether the trial is big enough to finish one honest test: a real chapter, a real report, a real export you can open. HumanPen gives you 100 free credits when you register, they never expire, and the same balance works across the web tools and the API.

What "free credits" is not: it is not a permanent free tier and not unlimited use. It is a starting balance — enough to test, not enough to run a whole thesis for free. Say it that way and nobody feels misled.

The test worth running

  1. Pick the worst chapter, not the easiest. The value shows up on the document you are worried about.
  2. Import the report you were given so the flagged spans set the scope.
  3. Rewrite, export, and open the file. The export is the product; a beautiful paragraph in a broken DOCX is not.
  4. Inspect five things: heading numbering, the widest table, footnotes, captions, page breaks.
  5. Re-check with the same report type and compare the same field. Note what moved and what did not.

How far 100 credits actually goes

You are billed for the words actually rewritten, not the size of the file. That is the whole reason a targeted pass is affordable: a 12,000-word chapter with 900 flagged words costs a fraction of a full-document rewrite, and the prose your supervisor already approved is never touched. If your first pass is small, the remaining credits are still there next month, because credits do not expire.

We will not invent a score promise, we will not suggest deliberate typos or grammar errors to fool a classifier, and we will not describe the output as "undetectable". Those claims sell once and cost you a viva. The trial exists so you can check the mechanics yourself before any money moves.
