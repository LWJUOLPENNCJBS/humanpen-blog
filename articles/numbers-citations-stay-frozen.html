Freeze the facts: numbers, formulas, citations and claim strength stay as they were

Charles Li / HumanPen · integrity · 8 Nov 2026

A rewrite that quietly turns "a 34% reduction" into "a significant reduction" has destroyed the sentence's value. HumanPen keeps numbers, units, formulas, citations, terminology and the strength of each claim exactly as they were while the prose around them is rewritten — and this is the one rule that makes the whole workflow defensible in front of a committee.

The test: take the rewritten paragraph, cover everything except the digits, symbols and in-text citations. That layer should be byte-identical to the original. If it is not, the tool overstepped.

Why claim strength is the sneaky one

Hedging is scientific content, not style. "The data suggest" and "the data show" are different claims, and a rewrite that "tightens" the sentence has changed what you assert. Examiners read for exactly this. When a tool refuses to touch hedges, that is a feature, not timidity.

How to verify in two minutes

  1. Search the digits. Use Find for each number in the rewritten section; every hit should match the original.
  2. Find the citations. The count of in-text citations should be unchanged; no citation should have moved to a different sentence.
  3. Check the hedging words. Scan for may, might, suggests, indicates, appears — the count should not drop.
  4. Check the units. Especially where a rewrite could reformat "mg/kg" into "mg per kg".
  5. Check term consistency. One concept, one term, throughout the chapter.

Why we will not teach the alternative

There is a genre of advice that recommends inserting deliberate typos or grammar errors so a classifier scores lower. It damages the writing, it does not survive a careful reader, and it is not something we will put in a guide. FAQ

Will the rewrite change my percentages?

It should not. Numbers, units, formulas and citations are kept exactly as they were; if a digit changed, that is a defect, not a feature.

Can it convert my citations to another style?

Citation Fix supports Vancouver, APA 7, IEEE, Chicago and GB/T 7714, and runs as a separate step — it changes format, not who you cited.

Does it weaken or strengthen my claims?

Neither. Claim strength is frozen: a hedge stays a hedge, and an assertion stays an assertion.

What if my field has very specific terminology?

Terminology is preserved. Check the export anyway, and treat any drift in a technical term as a defect to report.
