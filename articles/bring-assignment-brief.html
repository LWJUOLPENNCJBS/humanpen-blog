Bring the assignment brief beside the detector export

Charles Li / HumanPen

A rubric scores quality; the assignment brief states what the task asked for—structure, verbs, word count, required sections. Detector highlights often sit on the very sentences that follow the brief (“state three limitations,” “define the construct”). Without the brief on the table, you cannot show that a flagged span was task language, not free invention.

This is distinct from rubric-beside-export (marking criteria ≠ the prompt text), from syllabus-clause-before-score, from same-file-to-meeting, from ask-for-full-export, from two-paragraph-office-hours, and from peer-review-not-detector. Here the literacy move is concrete: bring the original brief/prompt next to the export.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Better order

  1. Print or open the original brief/prompt (LMS PDF or announcement text).
  2. Open the matching attempt export and, if useful, the rubric—three documents, not one mashed pile.
  3. Map flagged continuous-prose spans to brief requirements first, then to rubric rows.
  4. Say which brief line the span answers; keep attempt ID and product/version visible.
  5. Only then discuss whether the wording still needs human revision under policy.

Why the brief matters

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs wrote or approved the brief. Aligning a highlight to a required move is process literacy—not an excuse to leave poor prose untouched. Without the brief, every span looks like unexplained “AI style.”

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not advice that “the brief made me do it” clears a flag—explain, then revise if needed.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against a report while you keep brief requirements in view. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Brief + export (+ rubric)—three distinct sheets.
  • Rubric ≠ brief / prompt.
  • Map spans to required moves first.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
