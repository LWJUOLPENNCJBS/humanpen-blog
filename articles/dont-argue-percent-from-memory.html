Don’t argue the percentage from memory in office hours

Charles Li / HumanPen

Students often walk in saying “it was about thirty percent.” Tutors open the portal and see a different number—or a different attempt. Arguing a remembered percentage burns trust before you reach the spans. Open the export (or portal) for that attempt ID first; then talk.

This is distinct from coverage-before-percent, from email-badge-vs-portal, from confirm-attempt-id-first, from save-export-with-attempt-id, from two-exports-side-by-side, and from don’t-rewrite-only-intro. Here the literacy move is concrete: verify the on-screen % for this attempt before debating anything.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Better order

  1. Bring the named export (attempt ID in the filename) or a live portal tab.
  2. Say the attempt ID aloud; wait until both screens show the same card.
  3. Read the % / band from the open report—not from memory or a chat note.
  4. Only then walk flagged continuous-prose spans against the legend.
  5. If badge email and portal disagree, open both and separate AI-writing from similarity.

Why memory fails

Email badges, LMS digests, and full exports can show different rounding, different meters, or an older attempt. Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs work from the portal attempt in front of them. A remembered “about 30%” that does not match their screen makes every later claim sound slippery—even when you are honest.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not advice to refuse discussion until a perfect PDF appears—open what you have, politely.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against a report once you are looking at the same attempt numbers as the tutor. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Don’t argue % from memory.
  • Open export/portal for this attempt first.
  • Match screens, then spans.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
