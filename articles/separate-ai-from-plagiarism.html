Separate AI-writing flags from plagiarism / similarity %

Charles Li / HumanPen

Email badges and LMS portals often place an AI writing estimate next to a similarity / plagiarism percentage. They look like twin scores. They are not the same scale: one is about estimated machine-like prose; the other is about overlap with known sources. Mixing them in one sentence (“the detector says 40% so I cheated”) derails office hours.

This is distinct from two-paragraph office hours (lead with similarity, then AI), from badge-not-verdict, from threshold-not-proportion, from name-detector-version, from legend-before-rewrite, and from coverage-before-percent. Here the literacy move is concrete: name each meter separately—never mash AI-writing and plagiarism into one claim.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Better order

  1. On the export, point to the similarity / match % and its source list (if any).
  2. Separately point to the AI-writing band, legend, and flagged continuous-prose spans.
  3. In speech or email, use two short sentences—one per meter—plus attempt ID and product/version.
  4. Ask which meter the meeting is about first; do not answer both as if they were one number.
  5. If citations explain overlap, say that under the similarity heading—not as a reply to the AI band.

Why mixing fails

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs may open integrity policy for source match and a different workflow for AI-writing estimates. A single mashed percentage cannot be mapped to either. Separating the meters keeps authorship talk honest and keeps citation talk where it belongs.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee either meter will clear next time.
  • Not advice to ignore a high similarity list or a dense AI highlight map.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against an AI-writing report while you keep similarity findings in a separate notebook line. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • AI-writing ≠ plagiarism / similarity.
  • Two meters → two sentences.
  • Citations answer overlap—not the AI band by default.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
