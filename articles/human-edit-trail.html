Keep a human edit trail: version history beats empty “I didn’t use AI”

Charles Li / HumanPen

When an AI writing badge appears, group chats often jump to a one-line defense: “I didn’t use AI.” Tutors hear that line every week. A human edit trail—dated drafts, track changes, comment history, and a short note of what you changed after reading the report—is usually more useful than an empty denial.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Why denial alone fails

A badge is an estimator output on continuous prose, not a courtroom finding. Saying “I didn’t use it” does not map the highlights, does not confirm attempt identity, and does not show your revision process. Students in India and Southeast Asia often lose the room because they bring emotion without a file trail.

Schools that ask for process evidence are usually asking whether you can explain authorship of flagged spans—not whether you can shout louder in WhatsApp.

What a lightweight trail looks like

  1. Keep dated copies (v1, v2…) or cloud version history of the same assignment.
  2. After the report, list which flagged continuous-prose spans you edited and why.
  3. Preserve correct citations; do not delete the bibliography to “look human.”
  4. Bring the trail + full report PDF to office hours, aligned to the same attempt ID.

What the trail is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not permission to fabricate fake timestamps.
  • Not a substitute for your school’s formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports a document-level rewrite loop against a report while keeping citations and layout—useful as part of a visible edit trail, not as a magic denial. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Edit trail > empty “I didn’t use AI.”
  • Date drafts; note what changed after the report.
  • Align trail + export to the same attempt.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
