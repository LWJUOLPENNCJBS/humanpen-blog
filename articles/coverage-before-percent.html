Read coverage before you react to the headline %

Charles Li / HumanPen

The big AI writing percentage is the first thing people screenshot. The more important line is often quieter: what the report says it covered—continuous prose only, exclusions, legend notes. A high % on a narrow coverage slice is not the same case as a high % claimed over the whole paper.

This is distinct from opening the legend before you rewrite, from confidence-band-not-score, threshold-not-proportion, badge-not-verdict, light-band-uncertainty, and tables/appendices sitting outside the AI slice. Here the habit is: coverage scope first, headline % second.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Coverage checklist

  1. Open the full export (not a notification card).
  2. Find coverage / scope language: continuous prose? page range? exclusions?
  3. Read the legend for what unmarked text means.
  4. Only then interpret the headline percentage.
  5. Bring coverage notes + attempt ID to office hours.

Why the order matters

Students in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs often escalate from “80% AI” without noticing the report scored only the narrative body while tables, methods lists, or appendices were out of slice. Tutors hear a whole-paper panic when the estimator never claimed whole-paper coverage.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee a wide-coverage % will fall.
  • Not advice to hide out-of-slice sections to game a band.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net helps you work from a full report against your document while keeping citations and layout—useful after you understand what was actually covered. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Coverage before percent.
  • Narrow slice ≠ whole-paper case.
  • Legend + exclusions + attempt ID.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
