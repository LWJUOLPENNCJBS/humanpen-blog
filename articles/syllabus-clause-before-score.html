Read the syllabus AI clause before you weaponize a detector score

Charles Li / HumanPen

A detector badge is easy to paste into email. The syllabus clause that defines allowed tools, disclosure, and appeal steps is harder—and more decisive.

This note continues detector-report literacy. It does not promise clearance. It asks you to put policy next to the score before anyone narrates guilt.

Why the clause comes first

If the clause is silent on AI writing, that silence is part of the fairness record. Do not invent a rule from a percentage alone.

Bring attempt ID, coverage, and both tool exports if they disagree. Keep citations if a rewrite follows.

Four steps

  1. 1. Quote the AI / academic-integrity clause (or note its absence).
  2. 2. Align the badge with what the clause actually says.
  3. 3. Separate similarity findings from AI-writing findings.
  4. 4. Plan next steps as process—never sell a guaranteed pass.

HumanPen and remember

HumanPen (https://humanpen.net) can illustrate importing a report and editing flagged spans while citations stay. It does not sell rankings or clearance.

Remember: syllabus first; score second; disagreement stays on file.

Remember

  • Clause before score talk
  • Silence in policy is evidence too
  • Separate similarity vs AI writing
  • HumanPen is workflow help, not a pass
