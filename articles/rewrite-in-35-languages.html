35 languages: rewrite in the source language, not through English

Charles Li / HumanPen · languages · 8 Nov 2026

Translate-and-back is the most common way a careful draft loses its meaning. A Bahasa Indonesia paragraph goes to English, gets "improved", and comes home carrying English word order in Indonesian words. HumanPen's rewrite engine works across 35 languages, so the paragraph is rewritten inside its own grammar, register and connectors.

Thirty-plus, precisely: the rewrite engine supports 35 languages. Translation is a separate tool covering 12 languages, and citation-format correction (Vancouver, APA 7, IEEE, Chicago, GB/T 7714) is language-agnostic. Keep the three numbers apart when you describe the product.

What survives a source-language rewrite

  • Register. Academic Indonesian and academic Vietnamese use formality markers English does not have; rewriting in-language keeps them.
  • Connectors. "Namun", "tuy nhiên", "sin embargo" carry argument structure. Translation flattens them into "however" and back into something else.
  • Terminology. Field terms stay as your field uses them, not as a translation model guesses.
  • Numbers, units, formulas. Frozen exactly, in every language.

Where students actually get caught

The tell is a paragraph that is grammatically fine but logically foreign: sentences in the right language, argument in the wrong order. Supervisors notice this faster than they notice a detector score. It usually comes from a two-hop pipeline, not from the writer.

A workable order for a non-English thesis

  1. Write in your own language and finish the argument first.
  2. If a report covers the file, import it and rewrite only the flagged spans — in the same language.
  3. Export and read one rewritten paragraph aloud; if the rhythm is not yours, the rewrite over-corrected.
  4. Only then consider translation, and treat the translated file as a new document that needs its own check.

Right-to-left and CJK specifics

Chinese, Japanese and Korean drafts keep their punctuation conventions and their spacing rules in a source-language rewrite; right-to-left scripts keep direction and numeral shaping. This is a formatting layer, not a translation layer — and it is the layer that most often breaks when a tool assumes English input.

Honest limits

Language coverage does not make a report predictable. Detection models are trained unevenly across languages, so a passage can read very differently to a vendor depending on which language it is in. No tool can promise a result in any language, and we do not. What language coverage buys you is a rewrite that a native reader — your supervisor — will accept as your own writing.

FAQ

Does 35 languages mean translation?

No. The rewrite engine supports 35 languages; translation is a separate tool with 12 languages; citation-format correction is language-agnostic.

Will my Chinese punctuation change?

A source-language rewrite keeps CJK punctuation and spacing conventions. Check the export anyway, since punctuation is where automated edits most often drift.

Can I mix languages in one file?

Files are processed as documents; mixed-language files behave best when a passage is consistently in one language.

Does language coverage change detection?

Vendors train unevenly across languages, so behaviour varies by language. We do not promise a result in any language.
