Get an explicit chat ack for the due date—silence is not agreement

Charles Li / HumanPen

A proposed due floating in notes can look agreed when nobody replied. After you propose a due date, get an explicit yes in chat—emoji or one-word ack—because silence is not agreement. Use the same habit for rewrite deadlines after a flag review.

This is distinct from confirm-due-with-owner (ask and wait for a spoken or chat confirm), from due-beside-owner-in-notes, from confirm-owner-before-leave, from end-with-owners-readback, from capture-decision-in-chat, and from label-owner-on-each-action. Here the literacy move is concrete: require a visible chat ack on the due line before you move on.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Better order

  1. Post the due on the shared chat: “Rewrite §3 · Maya · due Fri EOD—please ack.”
  2. Accept ✅, 👍, “yes,” “ok,” or a counter-date as the ack.
  3. If no ack in the next minute, @mention once; don’t treat quiet as consent.
  4. Paste the ack snippet back into notes next to the due.
  5. Flag review: every rewrite deadline needs a chat ack before hang-up.

Why a visible ack matters

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs skim parallel chats. A due without an emoji or one-word reply is often unread. The ack turns the export rewrite deadline into a logged yes.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not a demand for a long reply—one emoji is enough.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against a report when rewrite deadlines carry a chat ack, not only a typed due. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Get an explicit chat ack for the due.
  • Silence is not agreement.
  • Flag review: ack rewrite deadlines before hang-up.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
