Stamp the timestamp before you rewrite a flagged span

Charles Li / HumanPen

Offices often rewrite first and reconstruct the timeline later. That habit erases the one fact that makes badge shifts interpretable: when the export was taken.

This note continues detector-report literacy. It does not promise clearance. It asks for a timestamp before any rewrite.

Why the stamp comes first

Classifiers react to surface edits. Without attempt ID, coverage, and export time on the same sheet, a lower badge looks like confession instead of process.

If two tools disagree after the edit, keep both dated exports. Disagreement is fairness data, not a race.

Four steps

  1. 1. Copy attempt ID, coverage, legend notes, and export timestamp.
  2. 2. Screenshot or PDF the portal page before editing.
  3. 3. Rewrite only the flagged span; keep citations.
  4. 4. Export again with a new stamp—never sell a guaranteed pass.

HumanPen and remember

HumanPen (https://humanpen.net) can illustrate importing a report and editing flagged spans with citations kept. It does not sell rankings or clearance.

Remember: stamp first; rewrite second; keep both exports dated.
