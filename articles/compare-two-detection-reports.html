Comparing two reports: same vendor, same type, same field — or the number means nothing

Charles Li / HumanPen · method · 8 Nov 2026

Two reports, two numbers, and a decision. The decision is only as good as the comparison. Run the rewrite from the report you were given, then compare like with like — three things have to match before a percentage means anything.

The three-match rule: same vendor · same report type · same analysed coverage. Break any one and you are comparing a thermometer with a barometer.

What to write down before the second report

RecordWhy it matters
Vendor and report typeSimilarity and AI indicators come from different models
Date and settingsVendors update models; institutions change thresholds
Coverage note (what was excluded)References, quotes, tables and appendices are often outside the score
Which passages were flaggedThe only comparison that tells you if your edit worked
What you actually changedOtherwise you cannot attribute the movement

Three comparison mistakes

  1. Portal badge vs downloaded PDF. Different runs, sometimes different coverage. Read the coverage note first.
  2. Whole-document number vs one chapter. A percentage over 100 pages is not the same claim as a percentage over 12.
  3. Similarity drop read as AI drop. Two scores, two readings; one moving says nothing about the other.

What a good comparison looks like

Not "it went from 62 to 31". Rather: "Of the nine flagged passages, six came back clean; three are still flagged, all in the methods section; the indicator moved from X to Y over the same coverage; similarity is unchanged; the export kept tables and footnotes." That sentence is defensible in a supervision meeting because every clause can be checked.

When the second report is worse

It happens, and it is informative. Check whether the vendor updated, whether coverage changed, whether your rewrite introduced repetition, and whether the flagged spans are the ones you actually edited. Then run a second targeted pass on what is still flagged rather than rewriting the chapter again.

No tool can promise which direction the number moves — detection is probabilistic and vendors retrain. What you can control is whether the comparison is honest, and whether the file you exported still opens correctly. FAQ

Should I compare percentages across vendors?

No. Different models, different training data, different thresholds. Compare within a vendor and a report type, or compare the flagged passages instead.

Why did my number go up after rewriting?

Check vendor updates, coverage changes and whether the rewrite introduced repetitive phrasing. Then look at which passages are flagged now — that is the actionable part.

How many re-checks are normal?

Usually one or two targeted passes. If a third is needed, the scope is probably too wide or the rewrite is over-correcting.

Does the report keep a history?

Your institution's system may; keep your own baseline file anyway, with vendor, type and date written on it.
