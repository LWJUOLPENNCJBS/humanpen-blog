Note what you changed after editing flagged spans

Charles Li / HumanPen

You revised the flagged continuous-prose spans. Do one more small step: write a short dated bullet list of what you changed and why. Bring that note to office hours with your draft trail. It is process evidence—not a guilt confession.

This is distinct from keeping a long human edit trail, from the pre-rewrite flagged-span checklist, from “rewrite loops are not guilt,” from “diff view is not guilt,” and from the two-paragraph similarity/AI script. Here the artifact is narrower: after-edit bullets of what changed.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

A lightweight after-edit note

  1. Date the note; record attempt / submission ID.
  2. Bullet each edited span (short quote or location).
  3. One line why: clarity, citation match, authorship explanation, etc.
  4. Say what you deliberately left unchanged (e.g. bibliography).
  5. Attach the note to the same folder as the draft trail.

Why tutors ask

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs often need a map from report highlights to your revisions. Silence or “I just rewrote everything” leaves them guessing. A dated change list shows authorship process without turning the meeting into a confession booth.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not a forced admission of misconduct.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against a report while keeping citations and layout—then you can jot what changed for office hours. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • After editing, note what you changed.
  • Dated bullets + attempt ID + draft trail.
  • Process evidence ≠ guilt confession.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
