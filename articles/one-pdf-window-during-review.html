Keep only one export PDF window open during flag review

Charles Li / HumanPen

Three PDF tabs feel prepared. Mid-sentence you click the wrong one and point at a span that never appeared on the scored attempt. During review, keep only one export window open—the PDF/HTML for that attempt—and close older drafts.

This is distinct from two-exports-side-by-side (deliberate compare of two scored runs), from mute-chat-during-flag-review, from dont-edit-while-screensharing, from freeze-draft-before-office-hours, from ask-which-attempt-before-discuss, from bring-printed-export-to-meeting, and from write-attempt-id-on-notes. Here the literacy move is concrete: one open export window = the scored attempt only.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Better order

  1. Close other draft PDFs and editor tabs before the slot.
  2. Open only the named export for the scored attempt; check the attempt id in the filename or header.
  3. If you need an older draft later, reopen it after takeaways—not mid-talk.
  4. Screen-share that single window; do not Alt-Tab through a stack.
  5. Note in takeaways which single file stayed open.

Why extra tabs misfire

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs follow your cursor. An accidental switch to attempt-1 while discussing attempt-2’s badge looks like “the highlight moved.” Usually you pointed at the wrong window.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not a ban on planned side-by-side of two scored exports when that is the question.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against a report after a review that stayed on one named export. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • One export PDF/HTML window during review.
  • Close older draft tabs before you talk spans.
  • Wrong tab ≠ “detection moved.”
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
