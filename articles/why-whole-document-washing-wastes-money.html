Whole-document washing: more spend, less traceability, same problem

Charles Li / HumanPen · scope · 8 Nov 2026

There is a comfortable instinct after a bad badge: rewrite everything, then nothing can be wrong. It is the most expensive option available, and it usually makes the document worse. HumanPen works from the report and rewrites only the flagged passages, which is the opposite bet.

The arithmetic: a 12,000-word chapter with 900 flagged words. A whole-document pass pays for 12,000 words and touches prose your supervisor already approved. A targeted pass pays for 900 and leaves the rest identical.

Three costs of the whole-document approach

  1. Money. You are billed per word; the ratio here is roughly 13 to 1.
  2. Voice. Rewriting clean paragraphs is how a chapter ends up sounding like three different people, none of them you.
  3. Traceability. If everything changed, you cannot say which edit moved the measurement — so the next report is a fresh puzzle instead of a feedback loop.

When a whole-document pass is actually right

If the document was machine-drafted end to end and the report flags most of it, a broad pass is honest. But then say so, expect a bigger bill, and check the export five times more carefully — because a document-wide edit is where formatting regressions accumulate.

The habit that replaces panic

Baseline, scope, export, re-check, record. Four steps, small and repeatable. It also means the next chapter is faster, because you already know which fields to read and which parts of a file break.

FAQ

Is a whole-document rewrite ever cheaper?

Not when the report flags a small share of the prose. Check the flagged word count before choosing a scope.

Will a targeted pass leave detectable patterns elsewhere?

It leaves clean prose alone. If the report did not flag it, rewriting it adds risk to your voice without adding information.

Can I rewrite chapter by chapter?

Yes, but keep one baseline and one comparison per chapter — splitting a file is where cross-references break.

What if the report flags whole sections?

Then the section is your scope. The logic is scale-free: edit what was flagged, freeze what was not, verify the export.
