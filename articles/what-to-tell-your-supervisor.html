Talking to your supervisor: the four files that answer the question

Charles Li / HumanPen · supervision · 8 Nov 2026

Sooner or later a supervisor asks how the text changed. The wrong answer is a defensive one; the right answer is a folder. A report-driven rewrite leaves a trail you can hand over, and the conversation becomes administrative instead of adversarial.

One sentence that works: "I imported the report you sent, rewrote only the flagged passages, and re-checked with the same report type — here are the before, the after, and the export."

The four files

FileWhat it proves
Baseline reportWhat the problem looked like before you touched anything
Rewritten document (exported)That the file still opens, with formatting intact
Second report, same typeWhat changed and what did not
Your draft historyThat the argument, structure and sources were yours

What not to say

  • "It's now undetectable." False, and it invites a test you cannot win.
  • "The tool guarantees it passes." No tool does; detection is probabilistic and vendors retrain.
  • "I rewrote the whole thing to be safe." That raises a bigger question than it answers.

If your policy requires disclosure

Then disclose, in writing, before submission: what tool, what scope, what the second report showed. Institutions differ; some treat editing support as ordinary, others require a declaration. Reading the handbook is not bureaucracy — it is the difference between a footnote and an integrity case.

If the second report still flags something

Say so, and say what you plan to do: a targeted second pass on the remaining passages, then a re-check. Supervisors respond well to a specific next step and badly to a promise about a number.

FAQ

Do I have to tell my supervisor I used a tool?

Follow your programme's policy. If it requires a declaration, declare in writing before submission and name the scope, not just the tool.

What if they ask for the AI percentage?

Give the number with its vendor, report type and coverage, and give the flagged-passage comparison next to it. A number alone invites the wrong conclusion.

Is rewriting flagged passages misconduct?

That is defined by your institution, not by a tool. What keeps you defensible is scope, disclosure and a verifiable trail.

Which file do they actually want to see?

Usually the exported document plus the baseline report. Bring all four; the ones they do not ask for are what end the conversation quickly.
