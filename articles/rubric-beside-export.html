Bring the rubric beside the detector export to office hours

Charles Li / HumanPen

Many office-hours talks collapse into arguing a badge percentage. Tutors also grade against a rubric or marking scheme. If you only wave the detector card and never open the assignment criteria, you cannot show where your draft meets the brief—or where a flagged span is actually required evidence, method, or terminology.

This is distinct from bringing the same scored file, from asking for the full export, from confirming attempt ID, from reading the syllabus AI clause before weaponizing a score, from two-paragraph office hours (similarity then AI), and from legend-before-rewrite. Here the literacy move is concrete: rubric + that attempt’s export side by side.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Better order

  1. Confirm attempt ID; open the matching full export (and the same submission file).
  2. Print or open the assignment rubric / marking criteria for that task.
  3. Map flagged continuous-prose spans to rubric rows (argument, evidence, methods, style)—not only to “AI %.”
  4. Ask which criteria the tutor wants discussed first; keep the export for span detail.
  5. Leave with a short note: rubric row + span + what you will revise under policy.

Why rubric + export together

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs often hold both academic standards and an integrity tool. A badge alone invites a binary fight. The rubric reframes the meeting toward learning goals; the export keeps attempt identity and highlights honest. Neither replaces the other.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not advice to ignore a marked span because “the rubric asked for it”—explain, don’t dodge.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against a report while you keep rubric goals in view. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Rubric beside export—not badge % alone.
  • Map spans to criteria, then to highlights.
  • Same attempt ID file + criteria sheet.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
