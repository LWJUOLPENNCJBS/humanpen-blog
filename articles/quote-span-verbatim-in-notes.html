Copy each flagged span verbatim into your notes with page or paragraph

Charles Li / HumanPen

Summarizing “that awkward sentence on page 3” feels faster. Days later the paraphrase no longer matches the report. When a flagged span comes up, copy it verbatim into your notes with page or paragraph number—paraphrase only after the meeting if you need a gloss.

This is distinct from print-span-page-numbers (mark numbers beside spans on the export), from quote-block-before-score, from flagged-span-checklist, from triage-flagged-sentences, from read-legend-aloud-before-spans, from write-attempt-id-on-notes, and from pin-export-window-during-review. Here the literacy move is concrete: verbatim quote + locator in the meeting notes.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Better order

  1. Keep attempt id at the top of notes; open the scored export.
  2. When a span is discussed, paste or hand-copy the exact wording.
  3. Add page / paragraph / line locator next to the quote.
  4. Add tutor comments under the quote—not instead of it.
  5. Paraphrase only in a separate gloss line after the slot.

Why paraphrase drifts

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs point at exact strings. A softened paraphrase in your notes makes the next rewrite target the wrong sentence—and looks like the report “moved.” Usually your notes moved.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not a demand to paste every span in the whole report—only those you discuss.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against a report when your notes still hold the exact strings that were marked. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Copy flagged spans verbatim into notes.
  • Add page/paragraph beside each quote.
  • Paraphrase later drifts off the mark.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
