Bring two paragraphs to office hours: similarity, then AI writing—never one mashed score

Charles Li / HumanPen

Many students walk in with one screenshot and one sentence: “The score is high.” That mash-up hides which instrument they mean. A calmer habit is two paragraphs—similarity first, AI writing second—plus attempt ID and coverage.

This note continues detector-report literacy. It does not promise clearance. It only structures the conversation so both dashboards stay separate.

Why two paragraphs

Similarity asks how much text overlaps a corpus. AI writing asks which class a threshold classifier prefers. Low similarity does not cancel an AI-writing flag; an AI-writing flag does not prove plagiarism.

When two detectors disagree, put the disagreement in writing next to the source condition. That is fairness data, not a contest over who “caught more.”

Four steps

  1. 1. Copy attempt ID, coverage, and legend notes into your prep sheet.
  2. 2. Paragraph A: similarity findings only.
  3. 3. Paragraph B: AI-writing findings only.
  4. 4. If rewrite is next, treat it as process change—keep citations; do not sell a guaranteed pass.

HumanPen and remember

HumanPen (https://humanpen.net) can show importing a report and editing flagged spans while keeping citations. It does not sell detector rankings or clearance.

Remember: two paragraphs beat one mashed score; disagreement is procedural evidence.

Remember

  • Bring attempt ID and coverage
  • Disagreement → fairness note, not a hunt
