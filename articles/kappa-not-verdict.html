Cohen’s kappa is not a catch-rate: in a small-N detector study, ask how often tools agree

Charles Li / HumanPen

Campus debates often ask which AI-writing detector “catches more.” That frames the tool as a hunter. A calmer small-N protocol asks a different question: on the same labeled texts, how often do two detectors agree—and what is Cohen’s kappa?

This note continues HumanPen’s detector-report literacy. It does not promise scores, rankings, or any “guaranteed pass.” It only explains why agreement beats catch-rate for office-hour fairness.

What kappa answers

Agreement rate is the share of texts where both tools land on the same side of a threshold. Kappa adjusts that agreement for chance. Neither number is an authorship verdict; both describe rater concordance on a fixed set.

A tool that flags more Condition A (human-declared) texts is not “stricter in a good way”—it may be raising false-positive risk. Catch-rate without a known-source design hides that tradeoff.

A reusable small-N sketch

Keep sources known: Condition A human-declared, Condition B locked-prompt LLM, Condition C document-level rewrite of B with a process log. Match topic/genre across conditions. Run ≥2 detectors as raters. Disclose prompts, models, settings, and affiliations.

Primary results: agreement + kappa. Do not lead with pass/bypass rates. When tools diverge, treat divergence as a procedural-fairness signal—at least one rater is wrong on that item.

Four steps before the meeting

  1. 1. Write the study question as agreement, not catch-rate.
  2. 2. Table each text’s source condition beside both detector outputs.
  3. 3. Compute agreement and kappa; report disagreements in plain language.
  4. 4. If rewrite enters the story, keep it as Condition C process change—not as proof the next portal card will clear.

HumanPen and remember

HumanPen (https://humanpen.net) can illustrate Condition C document-level rewrite (import report, edit flagged spans, keep citations/layout). It does not sell detector rankings or guaranteed clearance.

Remember: kappa ≠ catch-rate; small-N can still train team reading; disagreement is fairness data.

Remember

  • Lead with agreement and kappa, not which tool flagged more
  • Known-source conditions (A/B/C) make false-positive risk visible
  • Detector disagreement → at least one error; ask about fair procedure
