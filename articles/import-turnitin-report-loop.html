Import the report, rewrite only what was flagged, then re-check

Charles Li / HumanPen · workflow · 8 Nov 2026

Most students rewrite the whole chapter after one bad badge. The cheaper and more accurate path is to let the report decide the scope: import it, rewrite the spans it flagged, export the editable file, and re-check with the same report type. HumanPen reads a Turnitin or iThenticate report and rewrites only the passages that were flagged, keeping layout, citations and meaning where they were.

Why scope matters: you are billed for the words actually rewritten, not the file size. A targeted pass on 400 flagged words costs a fraction of a whole-document wash and leaves the prose your supervisor already approved untouched.

Step 1 — keep a baseline you can compare against

Download the report you were given (PDF or the vendor's own export) and do not overwrite it. Write down three things on it: the vendor, the report type, and the date. Without those three, the next number is not comparable — a similarity report and an AI writing indicator move for different reasons, and a report generated under different settings can shift without a single word changing.

Step 2 — import, do not retype

Uploading the report is what turns it into a target list. If you retype passages by hand you lose the mapping between "this sentence was flagged" and "this sentence I changed", and you will not be able to say what actually moved the measurement.

Step 3 — rewrite flagged spans, freeze everything else

ElementHandling
Flagged proseRewrite into your own cadence, add the specific detail the flagged paragraph was missing
Numbers, units, formulasKept exactly as they were
Citations and referencesKept exactly as they were
Terminology and claim strengthKept exactly as they were — "may" does not become "does"
Unflagged proseLeft alone; it is not the problem

Step 4 — export and inspect the file, not just the text

Open the returned DOCX or PPTX and look at the things a reader notices: table column widths, footnote numbering, figure captions, page breaks, slide text boxes and speaker notes. A workflow that hands you plain text back will cost you an evening of reformatting; a document-level rewrite returns the same file with its original styling untouched.

Step 5 — re-check and read the same field

Re-submit and compare like with like: same vendor, same report type, same field. If some spans cleared and two did not, the second pass is small and targeted. Repeat until the report no longer flags the passage you were working on, or until your programme's policy says the document is acceptable.

Step 6 — record what you did

Keep the baseline report, the rewritten file and the second report together. If a tutor asks how the text changed, a dated three-file trail answers the question in ten seconds; a cleaned-up folder with one final version answers nothing.

Common mistakes

  • Rewriting the abstract because the body was flagged. Fix what is flagged; a badge is not a verdict on the whole file.
  • Treating a pale highlight as proof. Low-confidence bands are the vendor telling you it is unsure; put them second in the queue.
  • Comparing a portal badge with a downloaded PDF from a different run. Check the coverage note before comparing numbers.
  • Adding deliberate typos. It damages the writing, does not survive a careful reader, and we do not do it.

FAQ

Do I need to upload the whole report or just the highlights?

Upload the report you were given. The tool needs the flagged passages in their document context to rewrite only those spans; a screenshot of the highlights loses that context.

What if the second report flags different sentences?

That is normal. Vendors resample and retrain. Compare field by field against your baseline, then run a second targeted pass on whatever is still flagged rather than rewriting everything again.

Does this work for a PDF report?

A PDF report is imported the same way as the DOCX one. What matters is that the flagged spans are readable, not the container format.

Will my references and tables change?

They should not. Numbers, units, formulas, citations and terminology are kept exactly as they were, and the returned file keeps its original styling.
