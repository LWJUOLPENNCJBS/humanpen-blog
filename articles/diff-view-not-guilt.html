Open the diff view before you treat a rewrite as guilt

Charles Li / HumanPen

Many offices treat a lower AI-writing badge after a rewrite as proof the first draft “was AI.” That leap skips the evidence you already have: the diff.

This note continues detector-report literacy. It does not promise clearance. It asks you to read what actually changed before you narrate guilt.

Why the diff comes first

A rewrite moves tokens. Classifiers respond to surface cues. Keeping citations and method paragraphs while clarifying one flagged span is process, not a confession.

If two detectors still disagree after the rewrite, record both exports with the same attempt ID. Disagreement is fairness data.

Four steps

  1. 1. Export before and after with attempt IDs.
  2. 2. Open the diff: list kept citations and untouched method lines.
  3. 3. Note which spans the student edited and why.
  4. 4. Only then compare badges—never sell a guaranteed pass.

HumanPen and remember

HumanPen (https://humanpen.net) can illustrate importing a report and editing flagged spans while citations stay. It does not sell rankings or clearance.

Remember: diff first; badge second; disagreement stays on the record.

Remember

  • Diff before guilt narrative
  • Keep citations through edits
  • Disagreement → fairness note
  • HumanPen is workflow help, not a pass
