An AI Writing Score Is a Threshold Call, Not a Proportion of Machine Text

A portal badge that says “18% AI writing” looks like a measuring tape. It usually is not. Most campus detectors return a classifier output under a display rule—often with confidence bands—not a verified count of machine-authored words.

This note is report literacy. It does not promise any score after revision.

What the number usually means

  • A model scored qualifying continuous prose and applied a threshold or band.
  • Lists, tables, short answers, and some appendices may sit outside the denominator.
  • Two tools on the same PDF can diverge because denominators and thresholds differ.

What it does not mean

  • That 18% of the file was written by a specific model.
  • That a rewrite will move the digit by a predictable amount.
  • That disagreement proves “caught” or “missed” authorship.

Fairer talk tracks

  1. Open the full PDF, not a messenger crop of the badge.
  2. Ask which passages were scored and what the grey/asterisk band means.
  3. When two detectors disagree, treat process fairness before crowning the harsher tool.

If revision is already decided, scope it to highlighted continuous prose on the matching upload. Importing a Turnitin or iThenticate AI report to rewrite only AI-flagged passages—preserving citations and formatting—is the narrow job HumanPen is built for. It does not predict the next digit.
