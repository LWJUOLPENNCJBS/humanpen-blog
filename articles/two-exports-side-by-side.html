Put two disagreeing exports side by side

Charles Li / HumanPen

Two tools, two percentages, one heated group chat. The useful move is not to argue from memory or from cropped WhatsApp screenshots. Put both full exports side by side, with attempt or submission IDs visible, then align legends and coverage before you debate any span.

This is distinct from “one detector is not enough,” from “a screenshot is not a case file,” from crops that hide IDs, from opening the legend before rewrite, from kappa-as-verdict, and from agreement-as-catch-rate. Here the practice is concrete: two PDFs, same table, IDs showing.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Side-by-side checklist

  1. Download both full exports (PDF or portal download)—not phone crops.
  2. Write down each attempt / submission ID next to the tool name.
  3. Confirm you are comparing the same file version / attempt.
  4. Open both legends: what does each color/band mean?
  5. Only then mark which continuous-prose spans overlap or diverge.

Why memory fails

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs cannot reconstruct two reports from your recollection of “one said 40%, one said 12%.” Without IDs and legends, disagreement may be different attempts, different coverage rules, or different display bands—not a verdict war.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee either tool will clear next time.
  • Not advice to shop detectors until one looks friendly.
  • Not a substitute for your school’s formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net helps you work from a full report against your document while keeping citations and layout—useful after exports are aligned side by side. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Two full PDFs + IDs > memory or chat crops.
  • Align legends and coverage before span debates.
  • Same attempt before comparing numbers.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
