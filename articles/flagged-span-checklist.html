Make a flagged-span checklist before you rewrite

Charles Li / HumanPen

When an AI writing report lights up continuous prose, the urge is to rewrite everything tonight. That panic pass often breaks citations, flips structure, and still leaves you unable to explain what changed. A short flagged-span checklist—which spans were marked, and what you plan to change in each—beats blank-page panic.

This is distinct from “rewrite loops are not guilt,” from triage of which sentences matter, and from keeping a long-term human edit trail. Here the move is narrower: write the list first, then edit against it.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

A lightweight checklist

  1. Open the full export (not a cropped badge). Note attempt ID.
  2. Number each flagged continuous-prose span (or paste a short quote + location).
  3. For each row: keep / clarify authorship / revise wording / leave (e.g. quoted methods language).
  4. Edit only against that list; preserve correct citations and layout.
  5. Save the checklist with the draft trail for office hours.

Why the list helps

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs can follow a span-by-span plan. A whole-paper panic rewrite gives them nothing to map. The checklist also stops you from “fixing” unmarked tables and bibliographies that were never in the AI slice.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not a demand to rewrite every highlighted word.
  • Not a substitute for your school’s formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level work against a report while keeping citations and layout—useful once your checklist says what to touch. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Checklist before rewrite > panic rewrite of the whole paper.
  • Span + planned change + attempt ID.
  • Do not trash unmarked sections to “look human.”
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
