Schedule the follow-up before you leave office hours

Charles Li / HumanPen

Many meetings end with a vague “email me when you’ve revised.” Without a time on the calendar, that follow-up dissolves into silence. Before you walk out, book the next check—even ten minutes online—to reopen the same attempt export and takeaways.

This is distinct from writing meeting takeaways, from one-question-per-meeting, from list-edits-chronologically, from note-what-you-changed, from same-file-to-meeting, and from ask-for-full-export. Here the literacy move is concrete: schedule the next check before you leave the room.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Better order

  1. End the content talk with one question answered or parked.
  2. Write your three takeaway lines (agreed / bring next / spans open).
  3. Ask for a specific follow-up slot—date, time, mode (office / Zoom / email deadline).
  4. Put it in your calendar with attempt ID and export filename in the event note.
  5. If the tutor cannot book now, send a same-day email proposing two times.

Why unscheduled “later” fails

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs juggle queues and marking. A floating “later” has no owner. A booked slot keeps the conversation on the same attempt timeline and prevents you from revising into a void.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not pressure to demand more of the tutor’s time than policy allows—ask politely for what fits.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against a report between scheduled check-ins. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Book the follow-up before you leave.
  • Unscheduled “we’ll look again” ≈ never.
  • Calendar note: attempt ID + export name.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
