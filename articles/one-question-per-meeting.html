Prepare one concrete question for a short office-hours slot

Charles Li / HumanPen

Ten minutes cannot absorb an entire detector PDF. Opening with “the percentage is unfair” usually burns the slot. Walk in with one concrete question—for example, whether this flagged span answers a required brief line—and leave secondary meters for email or a follow-up.

This is distinct from two-paragraph office hours (similarity then AI as two topics), from triage-flagged-sentences, from bring-assignment-brief, from dont-argue-percent-from-memory, from flagged-span-checklist, and from rubric-beside-export. Here the literacy move is concrete: one meeting → one sharp question, not the whole %.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Better order

  1. Pick one span (or one meter) that actually needs the tutor’s judgment.
  2. Write the question in one sentence; bring export + brief/rubric pages that support it.
  3. Confirm attempt ID; open the matching portal/export—do not recite % from memory.
  4. Ask the question in the first minute; take notes; book follow-up if more remains.
  5. Save the overall-badge debate for when you have a longer slot or written process.

Why one question fits short slots

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs often run walk-in queues. A single, well-framed question respects their time and gets an answer you can act on. Dumping the whole report invites a rushed, unhelpful “just revise everything” reply.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not advice to hide other serious issues—flag them for a second meeting or email.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against a report after you leave with one clear next edit. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • One short meeting → one concrete question.
  • Don’t open with the overall %.
  • Span/brief alignment beats a speech.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
