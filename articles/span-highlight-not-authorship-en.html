A span highlight is not authorship: separate the similarity dashboard from the AI-writing dashboard

Charles Li / HumanPen

People treat yellow/orange/red spans as authorship stickers: «It is highlighted, so a machine wrote it.» A calmer reading: the color marks a classifier’s confidence on a continuous-prose span, not a courtroom finding of who authored it.

This note continues detector-report literacy. It promises no score and is not a guide to «guaranteed pass.» It only shows how to read two instruments apart.

What a span highlight is saying

Most campus reports slice scored continuous prose into spans and paint each with a confidence color. The paint answers «which class does this surface statistics look like,» not «whose keyboard produced this sentence.»

Short lines, lists, formulaic methods sections, and additional-language writing often trigger false-positive bands. Rewrite changes surface statistics, but process and citation still need to be explainable—education questions are not answered by color alone.

Two instruments, one office hour

The similarity dashboard asks how much a passage overlaps a corpus. The AI writing dashboard asks which class a threshold classifier favors. Low similarity does not cancel an AI writing indicator; an AI writing indicator does not prove plagiarism.

Two screenshots side by side ≠ finished evidence review. In office hours, name which dashboard you are discussing before you negotiate the next review step.

Four steps before the talk

  1. 1. Open the full report: legend, coverage, low-confidence band, attempt/version—do not only forward a highlight screenshot.
  2. 2. Write similarity and AI writing as two separate paragraphs; do not crush them into «the score is high.»
  3. 3. If rewrite is required: prioritize high-confidence continuous prose that is flagged; keep citations and layout; do not believe a full-file rewrite locks the next card.
  4. 4. When two tools disagree, record that disagreement—it is a procedural-fairness issue, not a contest of who catches harder.

HumanPen and remember

For a document-level workflow—import the report, change only flagged spans, keep citations and layout—see HumanPen (https://humanpen.net). It does not promise what the next portal card will show, and it does not sell a guaranteed pass.

Takeaway: span highlight ≠ authorship; keep the two dashboards separate; a score is at most the start of a conversation.

Remember

  • Highlight color marks classifier confidence, not authorship
  • Similarity and AI writing are different instruments—state them separately
  • Two tools disagree → at least one is wrong; ask about fair process first
