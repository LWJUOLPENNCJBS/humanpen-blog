A phone screenshot is not a misconduct case file

Charles Li / HumanPen

When an AI writing percentage or badge appears, students often photograph the phone screen and drop the crop into WhatsApp or WeChat. That image feels like evidence. In office hours it usually is not. A phone screenshot of a badge is not a misconduct case file.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

What a screenshot usually misses

A crop shows a number or a colored pill. It rarely shows the full continuous-prose highlights, the detector name and version, the submission or attempt ID, the export timestamp, or whether teacher and student views match. Tutors cannot map your draft to a cropped chat bubble.

Students in India and Southeast Asia often lose the room because they bring a viral image instead of the portal export that the school actually logged.

What to bring instead

  1. The full detector export PDF (or official portal download), not a phone crop.
  2. The attempt / submission ID that matches the LMS row.
  3. A matching draft trail (dated copies or version history) for the same attempt.
  4. A short note of which flagged continuous-prose spans you can explain.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not advice to hide screenshots that your school already asked for.
  • Not a substitute for the formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net helps you work from a full report against your document while keeping citations and layout—useful when you prepare office hours with real exports, not chat crops. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Screenshot ≠ case file.
  • Full PDF + attempt ID + draft trail > cropped %.
  • Align everything to the same submission.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
