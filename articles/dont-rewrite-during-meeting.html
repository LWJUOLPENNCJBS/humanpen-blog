Don’t rewrite the draft live during office hours to “see if the % drops”

Charles Li / HumanPen

Panic typing while the tutor watches feels productive. It is not. The export on screen is for a frozen attempt. Live rewrites create a new file the report never scored, so you stop aligning spans and start guessing what a future badge might say.

This is distinct from don’t-rewrite-only-intro, from rewrite-loop-not-guilt, from same-file-to-meeting, from timestamp-before-rewrite, from one-question-per-meeting, and from note-what-you-changed. Here the literacy move is concrete: talk against the open export now; rewrite after, on a scheduled follow-up.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Better order

  1. Keep the matching submission + named export open; ask your one concrete question.
  2. Mark page/paragraph numbers; take three takeaway lines.
  3. Schedule the follow-up before you leave.
  4. Rewrite afterward against the checklist—not mid-meeting under pressure.
  5. If you must demo a tiny fix, label it clearly as post-attempt scratch, not the scored file.

Why live rewrite derails

Tutors in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs need shared evidence: the portal attempt. Mid-meeting surgery breaks that shared object. The slot becomes a writing workshop without a new detector run—and without answering the question you came to ask.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not a ban on jotting tutor suggestions—notes yes, full rewrite no.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against a report after the meeting, when you work from the scored attempt. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Don’t rewrite live to chase a %.
  • Open export = frozen attempt evidence.
  • Rewrite after takeaways + follow-up.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
