Name the detector product and version when you discuss flags

Charles Li / HumanPen

Saying “the AI detector flagged me” is almost never enough. Tutors need the product name and a version, build, or report/attempt date. Without that, you may be arguing about a different tool, an old LMS integration, or a re-run that changed the legend.

This is distinct from one-detector-not-enough, from confirming attempt ID, from asking for the full export, from putting two exports side by side, from rubric-beside-export, from timestamp-before-rewrite, and from legend-before-rewrite. Here the literacy move is concrete: name product + version/date before debating spans.

This note is detector-report literacy. It does not promise any score, and it is not a detection-evasion guide.

Better order

  1. Copy the product name exactly as shown on the portal or export header.
  2. Note version, build, model label, or at least the report date / attempt timestamp.
  3. Keep attempt ID with that pair so re-runs are not mixed.
  4. In email or office hours, lead with product + version/date, then the % and spans.
  5. If a second tool appears, name both—never merge them into “AI detection.”

Why vague names fail

Schools in India and Southeast Asia English-medium programs may switch vendors, enable LMS plugins, or refresh report templates. A screenshot % without product/version cannot be matched to policy, training, or a prior case. Naming the build is process clarity, not pedantry.

What this is not

  • Not a guarantee the next badge will clear.
  • Not advice to shop detectors until one looks friendlier.
  • Not a substitute for a formal integrity process if one is opened.

Where HumanPen fits

HumanPen at humanpen.net supports document-level revision against a named report once product and attempt identity are clear. No guaranteed next percentage.

Remember

  • Product name + version/date—not “AI detector.”
  • Pair them with attempt ID.
  • Two tools → two named lines.
  • Honest tools do not promise a clean next card.
