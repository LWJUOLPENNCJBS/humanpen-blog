Open the report legend before you rewrite a single sentence

Charles Li / HumanPen

A common rush move is to rewrite the brightest yellow span on a phone screenshot. A calmer sequence opens the full PDF or portal report first: legend, coverage note, low-confidence band, and attempt/version ID.

This note continues detector-report literacy. It does not promise scores or clearance. It only puts legend alignment ahead of rewrite labor.

What the legend is for

Colors answer “how confident is this classifier on this continuous prose span,” not “who typed the sentence.” Pale bands often mean uncertainty—not a free pass and not a conviction.

Without the legend, a crop can turn a low-confidence hint into a panic rewrite of citations, lists, or formulaic methods that were never the educational issue.

Four steps

  1. 1. Open the full report; write down attempt ID and coverage.
  2. 2. Translate the legend into plain language before touching prose.
  3. 3. Prioritize high-confidence continuous prose flags; keep citations and layout.
  4. 4. If two tools disagree, record the disagreement—do not chase a single card.

HumanPen and remember

HumanPen (https://humanpen.net) can illustrate importing a report and editing flagged spans while keeping citations. It does not sell guaranteed clearance.

Remember: legend before rewrite; attempt ID before screenshots; process over panic.
